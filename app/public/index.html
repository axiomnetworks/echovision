EchoVision

Watch this for me. EchoVision is a public prototype from Open Independence. Point your camera at something and ask about what you see: "watch the door," "find the milk," "tell me when the light turns blue." Instead of describing everything, it tries to stay quiet until it has an answer, then speaks it aloud.

How it works

The page keeps a rolling buffer of up to four short camera clips, about eight seconds in total, and sends recent clips to Meta's AI, which looks for the answer to your question. Analysis continues while a reply is spoken, so watching goes on during answers. Replies play aloud in your device's own voice. Each answer takes a few seconds, so this is clip-by-clip watching, not instant continuous vision. Brief events can be missed and answers can be wrong, so do not rely on it for safety.

No app to install and no account to create. It is free to try during this public test.

Built for Aivi

EchoVision comes from Open Independence, a one-person accessibility app studio. What proves out here is planned to become part of Aivi, their iPhone app that already answers spoken questions about what your camera sees, out loud. That integration is planned work, with no release date yet.

Before you start

Video clips from your camera or screen and your questions go through Cloudflare to Meta. Spoken questions are sent as audio to Meta. Meta's free-credit account warns content may be used for product improvement. Answers are spoken by your browser's built-in speech on your device; this prototype sends nothing to a separate speech service, though your device's speech may use network voices depending on its settings. This is not zero-retention.

Scene references and recent replies stay in this page and server session memory only, until Stop, Forget, page close, or server memory eviction. Page-close cleanup is best effort; provider records are separate. Forget does not delete provider records. No face identification, turn-by-turn directions, safe-route or obstacle-clearance guarantees. Only share media you have the right to share, without other people's private information.
